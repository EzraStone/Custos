§ 01

The question, and the harder one that replaced it

The specification asked whether the burst signature separates an agent from a chatbot using flow logs alone. That question cannot be answered honestly without asking a strictly harder one:

Does an agent separate from a chatbot using flow logs alone, after those flow logs have been degraded by aggregation and TLS connection reuse the way real VPC Flow Logs degrade them?

The specification budgeted two weeks and an AWS test environment for this gate. Answering the harder version in software first means the AWS environment gets built to confirm a result rather than to search for one — and had the result been a fail, that would have been known in a day rather than a fortnight.

G0 PASS

Separation margin +0.26, full recall, zero false positives, identical at both flow log aggregation intervals.

CI runs the experiment on every push and fails the build if it stops holding. It is the one result the whole company is contingent on, so it is not allowed to regress quietly between commits.

§ 03

Finding 1 — the specification's headline signal does not exist

The specification rated this Strong:

Burst of sequential model calls, monotonically growing payload size, sub-second gaps — source: flow log timing and byte counts.

It is not implementable. Two facts compose to destroy it:

  1. VPC Flow Logs aggregate per 5-tuple over a fixed interval — 60 seconds by default, 600 in the cheaper configuration a cost-conscious platform team will already have set.
  2. Every model provider SDK pools TCP connections, with a keep-alive around ninety seconds.

So twenty sequential model calls over one pooled connection inside one aggregation window produce one flow record, with the byte counts summed and the timing gone. Measured on the corpus's busiest agent at a 60-second interval: fewer than 0.5 flow records per model call. The individual calls are not in the data at any interval a customer will actually have configured.

This is the single most important thing the experiment established, and it would have consumed most of the two-week budget to discover by packet capture.

§ 04

Finding 2 — what replaces it is better

Cumulative egress-to-ingress asymmetry against model endpoints.

An agent resends its entire accumulated transcript on every step. Over an episode of n steps, bytes sent grow as n² while bytes received grow as n. A chatbot sends one prompt and receives one answer, so both grow linearly with a roughly constant ratio.

The full signal set

Five signals, each carrying a weight and a sentence a human can argue with. A signal that improves accuracy but cannot be explained in one sentence to the engineer who owns the workload is not worth having — every finding gets challenged by that person, and a confidence score loses that argument.

Against a bias of −3.2: a prior against calling something an agent. Most principals in an AWS account are not agents, and a classifier that forgets that generates a report nobody finishes reading.

What the classifier is forbidden from reading

Feature extraction is structurally prevented from reading principal names, enforced by parsing the module source rather than by convention. Real IAM roles are often named role/something-agent, and a classifier reading that would score well on this corpus while learning nothing.

§ 05

Finding 3 — the classifier is invariant to aggregation interval

Identical recall, precision, and margin at 60 and 600 seconds.

This is a commercial result more than a technical one. Had accuracy depended on 60-second aggregation, onboarding would include "reconfigure flow logs on your production VPCs" — a change request against production, a week of delay per customer, and a reason for a platform lead to say no. It does not.

§ 06

Finding 4 — what load balancer logs are worth, quantified

Without ALB access logs, recall falls from 100% to 60%. Precision stays at 100%, and both missed agents land in the review band rather than being dropped.

The two that degrade are the low-volume and short-episode agents — including finance-close-agent, which runs three times a day and holds write access to billing.

The agent that matters most is the one that degrades first.

That gives the onboarding ask a precise justification rather than a vague one: "Also give us load balancer access logs. Without them your most dangerous agent shows up as a maybe instead of a finding." Four fields are taken from those logs and nothing else — see what we take from an access log line.

§ 07

Finding 5 — two expected signals were measured and rejected

Both are recorded in the source under REJECTED, with the numbers that killed them, because both will be proposed again by someone who has read the specification but not the data.

Context growth

The specification's "monotonically growing payload size". Rejected twice over. It does not survive 600-second aggregation at all — every workload measures about 1.0. And where it is measurable, at 60 seconds, it fires hardest on the hardest negative: the multi-turn chatbot scores 5.67, above every genuine agent, because a multi-turn conversation accumulates context exactly the way an agent trajectory does. Using it would have cost precision on the workload type customers have most of.

Episode persistence

Length of a run of model-active intervals. At 600 seconds it degenerates into a proxy for call volume: the embedding service runs longer than every agent in the corpus. A signal that inverts under a configuration the customer chooses is worse than no signal, so it was dropped rather than weighted low.

§ 08

Finding 6 — call volume carries no signal at all

The highest-volume workload in the corpus is a negative: an embedding service making 13,606 model calls. The lowest-volume agent makes 55.

Any classifier that ranks by activity finds the agents nobody was worried about and misses the one they should be.

§ 09

Why this should be believed, and where it should not

The corpus is adversarial by construction

Four negatives exist specifically to defeat naive signals: a multi-turn chatbot that accumulates context like an agent, a RAG assistant that interleaves tool calls like an agent, a batch job with no inbound requests, and a CI pipeline producing exactly the "burst of sequential model calls" the specification leads with. Tests assert those properties hold, so the corpus cannot quietly become easy and turn a passing gate into an artefact.

The weights were fitted on this corpus

That is the honest limitation. What this establishes is that a separating signal exists and survives aggregation, and which features carry it. It does not establish that these specific weights generalise. The first real capture will move them, and the thresholds sit in measured empty space rather than at round numbers so there is room for that.

Synthetic traffic embeds assumptions

The byte model assumes roughly four bytes per token, standard MTU, and typical SDK pooling. Those are defensible and they are still assumptions. The AWS test environment is still worth building — its job is now to confirm the byte ratios hold against real provider endpoints, which is a one-week task against a stated prediction rather than a two-week search.

Read this before quoting any number on this page

No scan has run against a real account. Every figure here comes from synthetic telemetry generated by the A0 harness, and it is labelled that way in the source, in the sample report, and here.
